## Tuesday – The Remediation Problem at Hartwell Private Wealth

## Situation

Hartwell Private Wealth manages over 4,000 clients and has historically maintained a strong compliance record. Following an FCA thematic review, the firm was given 90 days to identify the full extent of weaknesses in its KYC documentation and provide a credible remediation plan. The issue appears to have originated from an 18-month-old CRM migration, where client documents were transferred but their required metadata was not consistently populated.

## Complication

The FCA found 47 incomplete or outdated records in a sample of 200, including cases where required reviews may not have been triggered after significant changes in clients' circumstances. Further investigation found around 1,100 client records with incomplete metadata, of which approximately 300–500 may require active remediation.

The problem is complicated by inconsistent processes between teams. Some relationship managers manually checked documents outside the CRM, while others relied on system-generated alerts that were no longer working correctly. Hartwell therefore does not yet know exactly which clients are affected or whether their suitability and compliance assessments remain valid.

## Question

How should Hartwell identify, prioritise and remediate the affected client records within the FCA's 90-day deadline while ensuring the underlying process and system weaknesses are fixed?

## Answer

Hartwell should establish a risk-based remediation process for the 1,100 affected records. Yusuf should first define clear criteria for categorising records based on factors such as missing documentation, expired KYC information and whether there has been a material change in the client's circumstances.

The highest-risk cases, particularly those involving changed circumstances or potentially compromised suitability assessments, should receive immediate client-level remediation. Lower-risk cases where the required documentation already exists should be resolved through internal document review and metadata correction, avoiding unnecessary requests to clients.

At the same time, Hartwell should identify the root causes of the failure and establish clear ownership between Compliance, Technology and Client Services. The firm should introduce mandatory metadata validation, reliable review triggers, documented processes and ongoing monitoring so that future CRM changes cannot silently disable critical compliance controls.

This gives Hartwell a way to meet the FCA deadline, focus resources on the highest-risk clients, minimise unnecessary client disruption and prevent the issue from recurring.
