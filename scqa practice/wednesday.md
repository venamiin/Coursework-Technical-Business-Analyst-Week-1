# Wednesday – The Waiting List Nobody Could See

## Situation

Merton Cross London Borough Council provides SEND services to local families and uses the legacy CareTrack system to manage EHCP assessments. The council has around 300 Children's Services staff, and the SEND team has historically been viewed as responsive. However, an inspection found that the council could not reliably evidence whether it was meeting the statutory 20-week EHCP assessment timescale.

Only fewer than half of the 214 cases completed in the previous year had reliable timeline data. The council also has concerns around sensitive-data handling, external health-partner data flows and the accuracy of its regulatory returns.

## Complication

There is disagreement over whether the core problem is CareTrack or the way the service operates. Fatima believes CareTrack is outdated and should be replaced, while Darren is concerned about the disruption, training and migration involved.

Sade's investigation revealed that the problem is much broader than the technology. Staff record information inconsistently, educational psychology uses a separate Access database, health assessments arrive through email, and important administrative tasks depend on individuals rather than controlled processes. As a result, the council cannot reliably see the true status or duration of its EHCP cases.

This creates potential statutory, legal, data-protection and reputational risks, particularly if the council's external SEND returns are also inaccurate.

## Question

What is actually causing the council's inability to reliably track EHCP timelines, and what needs to change before deciding whether to replace CareTrack?

## Answer

The council should complete a full discovery and process/data assessment before committing to a new case-management system.

Sade should map the end-to-end EHCP process as it actually operates, including all teams, systems, hand-offs and data sources. She should identify where information is created, who owns it, how it is recorded and where data is lost or duplicated. The council should also establish the true accuracy of its existing EHCP data and investigate whether its external SEND returns are reliable.

Only once these issues are understood should Merton Cross decide whether CareTrack needs replacing. If the main problems are inconsistent processes, poor data standards and manual hand-offs, simply buying a new system could reproduce the same problems in a more expensive platform.

The immediate priority should therefore be understanding and controlling the process and data, followed by a technology decision based on evidence rather than assuming that replacing CareTrack will solve the underlying problem.
